## v0.9 (2026-08-07)

Full paper draft v0.9-wip (2026-08-07). **Phase 0 of the review response** (`14-review-response-plan.md`) is applied: no number in this draft has changed, and no experiment has been re-run. What changed is disclosure and positioning, in four places. (i) §6.1 now states what the serial arm actually is, namely that mid-run injection is enabled and simply has no turn boundary to fire at because A's task is a single generation, and names boundary density as the axis the grid holds fixed; §6.10 carries the matching cross-reference, so the two sections now explain each other instead of appearing to contradict. §5 states the time-to-first-token anchor once, as a single rule for all three contracts. (ii) §2.4 gains a paragraph placing the contract against classical concurrency control, and §4.3 and §4.4 now name snapshot isolation and granular locking outright, with write skew carried into §4.3 and Limitation 3 as the boundary the design inherits. (iii) A new §7.5 states the trust model and tabulates what the contract does and does not defend against; Limitations moves to §7.6. (iv) The fairness claim of §2.4 is narrowed, and the two LangGraph strategies that map onto measured arms are named. **Phase 1 is also applied** (2026-08-07), and unlike Phase 0 it adds measurements, all in §6.1 and all on the deterministic harness. Three new experiments unfix the axes the head-of-line grid holds constant: `e2b_injection.py` varies boundary density at fixed task length and confirms that serialization's cost tracks the boundary interval (slope 1.041 against it, R² = 0.99995) while reproducing the grid's serial cell at k = 1 to within 0.5%; `e2c_retry.py` shows the reject penalty scales with the retry interval and stays inside one interval of it, so "never better than serial" does not depend on the 250 ms we chose; `e2d_nscale.py` shows the parallel row's flatness survives 4× the user count at a 39% cost, not 7×. Recomputing §6.1's statistics from the committed raw data also corrected a reporting error: 240 runs were measured but 236 produced an attributable first token, and the paper now says so and reports fit method, R² and dispersion. **Phase 2 is partly applied** (2026-08-07), covering the part that does not need a live model. §6.7 gains `--turn-scoping`, a per-turn prompt section that names the request a turn is serving, together with its ablation (`n3b_scoping.py`) — and, more consequentially, a correction: repeating the semantic-confusion measurement shows its rate is not a stable quantity (4% to 21% across repetitions of one configuration, against the 38% an earlier single run recorded), so the paper no longer reports a worst case and says why. Structural attribution was perfect in all sixteen runs. §6.11's suite figures are re-measured (3,551 passing, 35 skipped, no failures). **Phase 2 is now complete**, the live endpoint having become available. Four live experiments landed, and two of them correct the paper rather than merely extend it. §6.7 gains the live scoping arm: with scoping the model wrote another user's files in 0 of 40 turns against 26 of 40 without it, and completed its own work more often rather than less, so the semantic gap is mitigable and not intrinsic to sharing a transcript. §6.10 gains twelve repetitions per contract for the ranking check (38.2 s serial against 10.8 s parallel, with non-overlapping ranges), a direct measurement of the endpoint's own concurrency ceiling (eight concurrent requests cost 2.68× one, not 8×), and a history-length axis for the token premium that **refutes an earlier concession**: the premium does not grow with history (1.49× empty, 1.47× after five prior turns), because history inflates both contracts proportionally and cancels from the ratio. Limitation 1 is corrected accordingly. §6.4 gains a second operating point for shell-dominated work (effect share 0.094 with measurable lock wait, against 10⁻⁵ for file writes), which confirms the objection that shell is the effect that matters while showing the workload still sits below the knee. **Phase 3 completes the response** (2026-08-07): the abstract is rewritten around claims rather than an experiment list; Table 1's D4 column now records that the parallel contract's intervention is *narrower in kind* than serial's, cancellation rather than steering; §3 states how the axes were derived and how sparse the population is; and the placeholder references are resolved against their sources, which also **corrected a mischaracterization** — DeLM gates the *content* entering a shared context, not concurrency, so it is no longer cited as a fork-the-session system and the empty coordinate survives its nearest neighbour. Wording is unified (one term for the reject penalty, a stated rounding policy, a session-boundary footnote on the L = 30 s column), and two numbers are now sourced from the code rather than from memory (the replay buffer holds 5,000 events, not the ~1,500 turns previously asserted without a basis). ## v0.8 (2026-08-06) and earlier

v0.8 (2026-08-06) was a structural revision, not a measurement revision: the paper now presents **one system**. Earlier drafts described Coagora as a system built twice and drew roughly half their numbers from a separate exploratory codebase; that codebase is frozen and no longer referenced, and every number below is re-derived from raw data committed in this repository (`bench/multiuser/out/`). The comparison baseline is correspondingly sharpened: the serial contract is not a straw man but **the mode this system already shipped**, a multi-viewer shared worker that remains the default today, and the parallel contract is presented as an evolution of a working system rather than a clean-slate design. New in v0.8: an experiment on incremental replay for late joiners (§6.5), a measurement that motivates the conflict-scoped lock from this system's own data rather than by citation (§6.4), and an L = 30 s cell in the head-of-line grid (§6.1). Sections 5, 6.4, and 6.5 are new or rewritten; §6.6 through §6.10 are unchanged in substance with numbers re-derived. Prior drafts: v0.7 applied a style pass (em dashes removed outside verbatim quotation, acronyms expanded at first use, evaluation questions labeled RQ, references numbered in order of first citation); v0.6 re-measured on the current host; v0.3 through v0.5 added the design space, the collapse boundary, the lifecycle and live-model experiments, and the early exploratory phase whose results v0.8 removes.
